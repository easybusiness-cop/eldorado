## Objective and evidence limits

Build a repeatable local B2B funnel for Dunder Mifflin Paper & AI Corp. The company record identifies Scranton, Pennsylvania as headquarters and reports $44,994 MRR. The workspace contains no CRM export, customer list, win/loss history, product price list, gross margins, or fulfillment capacity. This plan therefore defines a low-cost validation sequence; it does not forecast revenue, prescribe discounts, or claim a conversion rate.

## Initial customer profile

Prioritize businesses in the Scranton service area with recurring office-supply purchases and a reachable procurement decision-maker:

- Professional-services and accounting offices.
- Property-management and facilities teams.
- Local multi-site businesses with centralized purchasing.
- Nonprofit and small-business offices with predictable replenishment needs.

Qualify an account before quoting: delivery locations, approximate monthly paper volume, current reorder process, incumbent-supplier pain, purchasing window, approval path, and delivery requirements. Do not collect sensitive personal data.

## Funnel and exit criteria

| Stage | Action | Exit criterion | Measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Target account | Build a named list from public business directories and existing, permissioned contacts | Account fits the service area and recurring-use profile | Accounts contacted by segment |
| First contact | Send a short, personalized email or call with a clear opt-out | Decision-maker responds or a follow-up date is agreed | Delivery, reply, and opt-out rates |
| Qualified need | Ask about volume, delivery, timing, and current friction | Need and buying window are confirmed | Qualified accounts / replies |
| Sample or quote | Offer a sample bundle or a transparent, no-obligation quote | Buyer confirms products, delivery, and approval path | Quotes / qualified accounts |
| First order | Confirm order terms and delivery capacity | Paid order is fulfilled | Orders / quotes; contribution per order |
| Replenishment | Ask for the buyer’s preferred reorder interval and method | Reorder reminder or standing-order permission is recorded | 60-day repeat-order rate |

## First 30-day pilot

1. **Week 1 — establish inputs:** confirm service radius, catalog, contribution margin by product, delivery capacity, and approved claims. Create a 30-account pilot list split across two segments. The list size is a workload limit for the experiment, not a market-size estimate.
2. **Week 2 — test the message:** contact each account with one concise value proposition: dependable replenishment, clear volume quoting, and an easy reorder path. Offer a sample bundle or quote only where fulfillment and margin permit.
3. **Week 3 — qualify and quote:** log buyer role, paper use, volume band, current supplier issue, purchase window, next step, and quote outcome. Follow up once on the buyer’s requested schedule.
4. **Week 4 — review:** compare segment-level replies, qualified needs, quotes, orders, contribution, and opt-outs. Keep, revise, or stop each message based on observed data. Do not scale paid spend until a segment produces positive contribution after acquisition and fulfillment costs.

Suggested cadence: initial email or call, one follow-up after three business days, then one final follow-up after a further week. Stop outreach after an opt-out or clear decline and follow applicable email and telemarketing rules.

## Scorecard

Track these formulas by segment and channel:

- Reply rate = positive replies / delivered first contacts.
- Qualification rate = qualified accounts / positive replies.
- Quote rate = quotes sent / qualified accounts.
- Win rate = first orders / quotes sent.
- Customer acquisition cost = attributable campaign and sales cost / new customers.
- First-order contribution = collected revenue − product cost − fulfillment cost − payment fees.
- Payback = acquisition cost / monthly customer contribution.
- Repeat rate = customers with a second order within 60 days / first-order customers eligible for 60-day follow-up.

Record the reporting window and source for every numerator and denominator. Set numeric performance thresholds only after the first complete cohort is measured. Reject any quote that falls below the approved contribution floor.

## Channel guidance and source

Start with permissioned referrals, targeted local outreach, and a simple quote page; these channels let the team validate the offer before paying for reach. If a tested segment later justifies paid B2B acquisition, LinkedIn’s official campaign guide recommends matching the audience, offer, lead form, and nurture path. Use a small capped experiment and compare cost per qualified account and contribution, not raw form fills: [LinkedIn B2B Lead Generation Campaign Guide](https://business.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/business/en-us/amp/marketing-solutions/images/lms-b2b-resources-hub/pdfs/B2B-Lead-Generation-Campaign-Guide.pdf?src=direct%2Fnone&veh=direct%2Fnone).
